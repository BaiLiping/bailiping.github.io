The compressor

Rotating blades squeeze incoming air. The turbine supplies the power through a shared shaft.

Higher-pressure air enters the combustor, where fuel is added and burned.

Same mechanism: a turbine powers a compressor. Different output: thrust or crankshaft power.

Shared machinery. Different jobs.

Both burn fuel in compressed air. In both, hot gas spins a turbine that drives a compressor. The useful output leaves the engines in different ways.

Comparison of a jet engine and a turbocharged piston engine
What to followJet engineTurbocharged car
Useful outputThrust from accelerating air backward.Crankshaft power from gas pushing the pistons.
CombustionContinuous, in a separate combustor.Repeated, inside each cylinder.
CompressionRotating compressor stages squeeze the air.The turbo compresses intake air; the piston compresses it further.
Turbine's jobDrive the compressor; a turbofan also drives a fan.Drive the turbo compressor using the cylinders' exhaust.
Mechanical connectionA shaft connects each turbine to its compressor or fan.The turbo shaft is separate from the engine's crankshaft.

A turbofan is still a jet engine. A turboprop or turboshaft uses related gas-turbine machinery to deliver shaft power; those variants are outside this demo.

Sources & model notes

How to read the demo

These are simplified teaching cutaways, shown in slow motion. Dots trace gas paths; their spacing and speed are not measurements of pressure, temperature, or flow. Green shafts show mechanical power, not gas passages.

Power demand changes an illustrative operating state. Both rotors respond gradually. Car boost lags a change in demand because the turbo needs time to speed up. The response bars are normalized within each engine; they are not comparable performance measurements. Their time constants are animation choices.

Slow motion changes playback speed only. Pause freezes both the mechanism and its response. Selecting a stroke pauses at that stroke's midpoint. The car is a four-stroke petrol engine: intake, compression, power, exhaust over two crankshaft revolutions (720°). The simplified valve timing omits overlap; a real engine's timing and ignition vary.

The turbo and crankshaft rotate independently. The intercooler removes heat from compressed intake air. In the jet, combustion stays continuous and all stages operate together. The optional turbofan view schematically shows a separate fan turbine and shaft; blade counts and geometry are illustrative.
