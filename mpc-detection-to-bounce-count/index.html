MPC Detection to Bounce Count

A resolved multipath component can provide four observables: a delay (a path length), an arrival angle at the UE, a departure angle at the BS, and a path loss. With known BS and UE positions and headings, these measurements become global geometric constraints. The goal is to identify the physical route behind each MPC detection and report its bounce count. Section 2 tests LoS and reflection sequences against a known map; Section 3 instead bootstraps virtual anchors, incidence points, and walls when the map is unknown.

Context. The theory uses the same kind of per-path tuple that a SAGE estimator can produce, but every scene and number on this page is illustrative rather than a replay of measured SAGE output. The current Gaussian_Splatting_Test benchmark consumes synthetic ray-tracer path observables with world-aligned, translation-only pose states. The virtual-anchor reading follows E. 01Measurement

The channel estimator resolves a multipath component into geometric and radiometric observations. Read the delay and angles first, then use the power–delay profile and path-loss factors to understand which physical route produced them.

1.1The resolved path tuple

For one multipath component the channel estimator (SAGE, ESPRIT, …) returns a tuple (τ, φ, ψ, PL). Each element has its own geometric meaning:

delay τ → a length L = cτ
angle φ (AoA at the UE)
angle ψ (AoD at the BS)
path loss → a calibrated path-gain / loss observation

1.3What changes path loss beyond distance

Four effects explain why paths with similar travel distance can arrive with very different power.

01 · eventInteraction loss at each bounce

Paths with the same travel distance span ~20 dB of excess loss. What differs is what they hit—which surface, how many times, and whether the interaction is specular or diffuse.

02 · surfaceMaterial and incidence angle

Polarization exposes the difference: on matched reflected paths, half of all matched pairs differ by more than 3 dB between polarizations.

03 · textureSmall-scale roughness

Rough-surface and small-scale effects redistribute energy and can weaken the coherent component even when the large-scale geometry is unchanged.

04 · visibilityBlockage and shadowing

Some clusters vanish for whole stretches of frames in the delay–frame waterfall while the geometry barely changes.

Read it. Ray-casting through the known map first verifies that the BS↔UE segment is unobstructed. Known array headings rotate the measured local angles into the global frame. A LoS MPC must then satisfy three gates at once: AoD points from BS to UE, reverse AoA points from UE to BS, and cτ = ‖UE−BS‖. When they agree, there is no incidence point and the bounce count is zero.

Read it. The candidate order A→B comes from the known map. Mirror BS in A to obtain VA¹, then mirror VA¹ in B to obtain VA². Trace UE→VA² to wall B for P₂; fold toward VA¹ and intersect wall A for P₁. The resulting BS→P₁→P₂→UE path predicts one tuple (L₂, φ, ψ). A match accepts this two-wall sequence and reports bounce count two—without any lower-order measurement.

Read it. For candidate A→B→C, mirror the known BS successively to VA¹, VA², and VA³. Starting at UE, intersect the line to VA³ with C for P₃, then fold toward VA² to get P₂ on B and toward VA¹ to get P₁ on A. Read it. For R→L→R, the image source moves right, left, then right as the known BS is mirrored through the ordered wall sequence. Folding the UE→VA³ line returns P₃ on R, P₂ on L, and P₁ on R. 3.6Two-bounce ambiguity: motion alone need not recover the two walls

A composite virtual anchor identifies an ordered reflection transform, not necessarily its individual reflecting planes. For two nonparallel walls meeting at c, rotate both walls by the same angle about c. Their relative angle—and therefore both ordered two-reflection transforms—stay fixed. Every known UE pose keeps exactly the same delay, AoA, and AoD, while the two incidence points and individual walls move.

What motion does not resolve. Two pose-specific candidate curves do not generically provide a unique transverse crossing in this construction; they share the same ambiguity orbit. Finite wall support, visibility, a separately observed one-bounce prefix, or a calibrated incidence-dependent path-gain model can add the missing information.
