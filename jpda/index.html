TRACKING / ASSOCIATION / UNCERTAINTY

One return.
Two plausible targets.

Joint probabilistic data association (JPDA) weighs complete, feasible assignments before updating each target. Explore what changes when measurements can be clutter, targets can be missed, and nobody gets to claim the same return twice.

Based on Fortmann, Bar-Shalom & Scheffe (1983) and Bar-Shalom, Daum & Huang (2009).

01 / MODEL

A small, explicit association problem

This is a synthetic Cartesian teaching example with two established point targets and a fixed measurement set. Target states and measurements are 2D positions; H = I. Each target independently generates at most one return with probability PD. Clutter is an independent homogeneous Poisson process with spatial intensity λ. There are no births, deaths, track management, or temporal trajectory simulation.

All candidate measurements are considered: PG = 1 is the large-gate simplification used in the 1983 paper’s Section III and footnote 5. The drawing window is only a viewport. Its boundary and the dashed contours do not gate measurements or change the likelihood.

02 / JOINT EVENTS

Normalize complete explanations of the scan

Let at = 0 denote a missed detection and at = j denote target t generating measurement j. Positive assignments must be distinct. Any unassigned measurement is clutter. With predicted measurement mean μt and innovation covariance St = Pt− + R:

\[\ell_{tj}=\frac{P_{D,t}\,\mathcal N(z_j;\mu_t,S_t)}{\lambda},\qquad w(a)=\prod_{t:a_t=0}(1-P_{D,t})\prod_{t:a_t>0}\ell_{t,a_t},\qquad p(a\mid Z)=\frac{w(a)}{\sum_{a'\in\mathcal A}w(a')} .\]

The likelihood ratio comes from the 2009 paper’s (38); the joint weight is the parametric Poisson-clutter form in 1983 (3.18) and 2009 (47), after removing a common λm factor. No additional clutter-count factorial is inserted. The demo enumerates all feasible events and normalizes their weights in log space.

03 / MARGINALS & UPDATE

Average the branches—and keep their spread

\[\beta_{tj}=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A:a_t=j}p(a\mid Z),\qquad \hat x_t^+=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\beta_{tj}\hat x_{tj},\qquad P_t^+=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\beta_{tj}\left[P_{tj}+(\hat x_{tj}-\hat x_t^+)(\hat x_{tj}-\hat x_t^+)^\top\right].\]

The missed-detection branch retains the prior. Each measurement branch uses its ordinary Kalman correction. These mixture moments are equivalent to the innovation and covariance formulas in 2009 (39)–(44). Marginalization follows 1983 (3.19)–(3.20) and 2009 (51).

The lab computes exact association sums for this scan under its independent Gaussian prediction model, then shows the decoupled JPDAF Gaussian update. A recursive JPDA filter approximates the resulting mixture; this is not an exact multitarget posterior over all time. Separate Gaussian tracks can move together under persistent ambiguity (track coalescence). Maintaining cross-covariances, as in JPDACF, is beyond this lab.
