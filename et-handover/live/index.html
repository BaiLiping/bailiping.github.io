    What to watch for

    ① Birth. The target first becomes visible to A. A creates the track, assigns local UID 3, and becomes its owner — the label is (A, 3). ② and ③ Prior handover. As the predicted track approaches B's (then C's) region, the handover score \(\Lambda\) crosses the threshold and A sends that neighbor one prior packet. The receiver starts a shadow track — A keeps the original; nothing is relinquished. ④ Shadow pruning. The target leaves B, so B quietly drops its shadow copy and tells the owner. No ownership ceremony is needed — B was only a shadow holder. ⑤ Ownership transfer. When A itself loses sight, ownership moves to the best-scoring shadow holder via a two-message handshake. A retains authority until the acknowledgment arrives. C issues a fresh local UID, and the label changes from (A, 3) to (C, 7). ⑥ Exit. C prunes the track when the target leaves the network.

    The four variants share the event protocol. H sends no further evidence. HM sends the selected non-null group to the owner; HL sends a likelihood message; HP sends a full local posterior. HM, HL, and HP return fused posteriors. The toy illustrates traffic and labels, not a numerical implementation of GrBP or its fusion rules.

    The deterministic measurement dots illustrate the detection model \(\mu=\rho A_\kappa\delta\), so they thicken near a station and thin at FOV boundaries. The dashed ghost ellipse that appears ahead of the target marks the predicted pose — the density the receiver's shadow prior is initialized from, which is why it reaches the border before the target does.

    The trigger: a handover score you can drag

    When should the owner speak up? The paper gates every handover on one quantity — the number of detections the receiving station would be expected to make of this track:

    \[\Lambda_{t\to r}=\mathbf 1\{p_t\ge p_{\rm th}\}\int\mu_{m,r}(\bm x,\bm E)f_t(\bm x,\bm E)\,d\bm x\,d\bm E\]

    The score uses the expected detection count: \(\Lambda\) is the mean count that the receiving station would see, averaged over the predicted density and gated on track confidence. Drag the target below and watch the three factors — the score only clears the threshold when the object is both confident and genuinely entering the receiver's view.
