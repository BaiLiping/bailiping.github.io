What is computed here

A single wall lies in the yz-plane at x = 0. Initially Tx = (−5, 5, 0) m and Rx = (−10, −5, 0) m, following the geometry in Fig. 3.4. The finite wall is an explicitly chosen teaching approximation: y,z ∈ [−30,30] m. A 180 × 180 midpoint grid evaluates Eq. (3.11), weights each point by its area, and bins its power by receiving azimuth and delay. Angles are measured toward the wall; elevation is integrated out. Nodes remain on the same side; the specular point stays on the wall.

Only the first 100 ns of excess delay are plotted; all wall samples contribute to the displayed angular moments and mean excess delay. Histogram interpolation and a small nonnegative display filter reduce bin artifacts. Metrics use the unfiltered surface weights. These are numerical illustrations, not reproductions of the dissertation's measured results or tables.

Normalization is deliberately explicit

The thesis's kαR is retained in the displayed physical equation. The plots compare shape: at each geometry and αR, each plotted spectrum is peak-normalized for S = 1 and then multiplied by S². The unknown common normalization cancels. Therefore, the display is not an absolute link budget, not dBm, and not evidence that absolute diffuse power changes in a particular way with αR. Setting S = 0 removes diffuse power; deterministic paths can still exist.

Random-process illustration

The plotted bins are independent complex-Gaussian coefficients with variance proportional to the finite delay-bin power. Gaussianity is a simulation choice supported by [T2]; Chapter 3's first and second moments alone do not specify a complete probability distribution. No pulse convolution, antenna pattern, phase-coherent waveform, or localization estimator is simulated.

Do not over-read the model

A finite αR produces directional diffuse scattering, not the separate deterministic specular component. S is an amplitude scaling parameter, not a calibrated RMS height or a general diffuse/specular energy split. The selected model does not explicitly describe height variations, nearby objects, diffraction, penetration, or multiple bounces. The thesis does not verify a double-exponential soft onset with this surface model. General geometry may shift the diffuse angular peak away from the specular direction; the centered far-field approximation is not valid everywhere.

The thesis primarily treats DM as self-interference for localization. Exploiting position information in its statistics is left for future research; this presentation does not claim an implemented diffuse-only positioning result.
