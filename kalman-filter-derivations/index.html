Kalman filter / consolidated derivations

One filter. Four families.

Group by the object being solved for—not by every name for the same algebra. Each family has an idea page followed by a complete equation sheet.

Shared setup / read once

The model and the common recursion

Model and assumptions

\[\begin{aligned}x_k&=F_kx_{k-1}+B_ku_k+w_k,\\z_k&=H_kx_k+v_k,\\w_k&\sim\mathcal N(0,Q_k),\quad v_k\sim\mathcal N(0,R_k).\end{aligned}\]

Gaussian initial state; known controls. Noises are independent across time, of each other and of the initial state.

\(x_k\in\mathbb R^n\), \(z_k\in\mathbb R^r\). \(F\): transition, \(H\): observation matrix. \(m\): estimate, \(P\): error covariance. Superscripts − / + mean before / after the measurement.

Inverse formulas assume \(P_k^-\succ0\), \(R_k\succ0\), \(Q_k\succeq0\); \(\|a\|_W^2=a^\mathsf TWa\).

Prediction and correction

\[\begin{aligned}m_k^-&=F_km_{k-1}^++B_ku_k,\\P_k^-&=F_kP_{k-1}^+F_k^\mathsf T+Q_k.\end{aligned}\]

Suppress k for a single correction:

\[\begin{aligned}\nu&=z-Hm^-,\quad S=HP^-H^\mathsf T+R,\\K&=P^-H^\mathsf T S^{-1},\\m^+&=m^-+K\nu,\\P^+&=P^--KSK^\mathsf T.\end{aligned}\]

\(\nu\): innovation, \(S\): its covariance, \(K\): Kalman gain.

The four families explain the same correction after this shared prediction. Gaussian assumptions give \(p(x\mid z)=\mathcal N(m^+,P^+)\); only second moments suffice for affine/LMMSE optimality.

Family 01 / key idea

Gaussian Bayes

Key equations →

Idea. Compute a probability distribution. Propagate the old posterior through the dynamics, then multiply the prediction by the new likelihood. Gaussian distributions are closed under both operations.

\[p_k^-(x)=\int p(x\mid x')p_{k-1}^+(x')\,dx',\qquad p_k^+(x)\propto p(z_k\mid x)p_k^-(x).\]

One operation, three algebraic views

Completing the square reads off the mean and covariance. Information form adds the quadratic coefficients. Joint conditioning reads off the conditional Gaussian blocks.

What has been merged

HMM forward recursion and recursive Bayesian filtering are the same predict–update schedule. GP conditioning uses the same Gaussian identity; an arbitrary GP need not have an exact finite-dimensional Markov realization.

Distinctive output: the entire posterior, not only an estimate. This is the exact Bayesian filter under the stated model.

Family 01 / one-page equation summary

Gaussian Bayes

← Key idea

For one update: \(x\sim\mathcal N(m^-,P^-)\), \(z=Hx+v\), \(v\sim\mathcal N(0,R)\), with independent errors; \(P^-\succ0,\ R\succ0\).

1 · Multiply Gaussian factors

\[\begin{aligned}p^+(x)&\propto p^-(x)p(z\mid x),\\-\log p^+(x)&=\tfrac12\|x-m^-\|_{(P^-)^{-1}}^2\\&\quad+\tfrac12\|z-Hx\|_{R^{-1}}^2+c.\end{aligned}\]

2 · Collect information

\[\begin{aligned}J^+&=(P^-)^{-1}+H^\mathsf T R^{-1}H,\\h^+&=(P^-)^{-1}m^-+H^\mathsf T R^{-1}z.\end{aligned}\]

Precision and information vector: the information-form update.

3 · Complete the square

\[\begin{aligned}m^+&=(J^+)^{-1}h^+,\quad P^+=(J^+)^{-1},\\p^+(x)&\propto e^{-\frac12(x-m^+)^\mathsf T J^+(x-m^+)}.\end{aligned}\]

4 · The same answer by conditioning

\[\begin{aligned}S&=HP^-H^\mathsf T+R,\\m^+&=m^-+P^-H^\mathsf T S^{-1}(z-Hm^-),\\P^+&=P^--P^-H^\mathsf T S^{-1}HP^-.\end{aligned}\]

\(c\) is independent of \(x\). The conditional covariance is a Schur complement.

Family 02 / key idea

Minimum-MSE estimation

Key equations →

Idea. Choose the gain, not a density. Among affine corrections of the prediction, make the mean-square error as small as possible.

\[\hat x(K)=m^-+K\nu,\qquad K_* = \arg\min_K\mathbb E\|x-\hat x(K)\|^2.\]

Two proofs of the same optimum

Orthogonality: the remaining error is uncorrelated with the innovation. Covariance minimization: differentiate the trace of the error covariance. Both produce the same normal equation for the gain.

Why innovations and Wiener filtering belong here

Project onto the past observations first. The innovation is the new orthogonal component, so only that component needs to be added. For Gaussian errors, minimum error entropy is another reading of the same covariance optimum.

Assumption advantage: finite second moments and the required zero cross-covariances suffice for affine/LMMSE optimality. Gaussianity is needed to identify this with the unrestricted MMSE estimator.

[2] Kalman (1960) · [3] Kailath (1968) · [10] Kalata & Priemer (1979)05 / 14

Family 02 / one-page equation summary

Minimum-MSE estimation

← Key idea

Let \(e^-=x-m^-\), \(\nu=z-Hm^-\), \(S=HP^-H^\mathsf T+R\); \(e^-\) and measurement noise are zero-mean and uncorrelated, with covariances \(P^-\) and \(R\), and \(S\succ0\). Here \(P^+\) denotes error covariance, not necessarily the exact conditional covariance.

1 · Error for an arbitrary gain

\[\begin{aligned}e(K)&=e^--K\nu,\\\mathbb E[e^-\nu^\mathsf T]&=P^-H^\mathsf T.\end{aligned}\]

2 · Orthogonality gives the gain

\[\begin{aligned}\mathbb E[e(K_*)\nu^\mathsf T]&=0,\\K_*S&=P^-H^\mathsf T,\\K_*&=P^-H^\mathsf T S^{-1}.\end{aligned}\]

3 · Equivalent covariance calculation

\[\begin{aligned}P(K)&=(I-KH)P^-(I-KH)^\mathsf T\\&\quad+KRK^\mathsf T,\\\nabla_K\operatorname{tr}P(K)&=2(KS-P^-H^\mathsf T).\end{aligned}\]

4 · Certify the minimum; update

\[\begin{aligned}P(K)-P(K_*)&=(K-K_*)S(K-K_*)^\mathsf T\succeq0,\\m^+&=m^-+K_*\nu,\\P^+&=P^--K_*SK_*^\mathsf T.\end{aligned}\]

Family 03 / key idea

Weighted least squares

Key equations →

Idea. Solve for a state vector. Penalize disagreement with the prediction and with the observation, each weighted by the inverse uncertainty. The solution is a quadratic least-squares problem.

\[\phi(x)=\tfrac12\|x-m^-\|_{(P^-)^{-1}}^2+\tfrac12\|z-Hx\|_{R^{-1}}^2.\]

Statistical interpretations

MAP: this is the negative Gaussian log posterior; its mode equals its mean. BLUE: the same generalized least-squares algebra applies to independent, unbiased observations of a fixed state. The sampling assumptions differ.

Algorithms, not additional principles

RLS updates the normal equations as observations arrive; the ordinary static case has \(F=I,Q=0\). Square-root / QR solves the whitened system without forming its normal matrix. See the implementation page.

Important distinction: BLUE requires a random unbiased observation of the fixed state. A fixed Bayesian prior mean is not unbiased for every possible state.

[4] Loeliger et al. (2007) · [5] Aitken (1936) · [8] Kaminski, Bryson & Schmidt (1971)07 / 14

Family 03 / one-page equation summary

Weighted least squares

← Key idea

For one update: \(x\sim\mathcal N(m^-,P^-)\), \(z=Hx+v\), \(v\sim\mathcal N(0,R)\), with independent errors; \(P^-\succ0,\ R\succ0\).

1 · Objective

\[\begin{aligned}\phi(x)&=\tfrac12\|x-m^-\|_{(P^-)^{-1}}^2\\&\quad+\tfrac12\|z-Hx\|_{R^{-1}}^2.\end{aligned}\]

The Gaussian MAP objective. Its Hessian is the posterior precision.

2 · Normal equations and curvature

\[\begin{aligned}J^+&=(P^-)^{-1}+H^\mathsf T R^{-1}H,\\h^+&=(P^-)^{-1}m^-+H^\mathsf T R^{-1}z,\\\nabla\phi(x)&=J^+x-h^+=0,\\m^+&=(J^+)^{-1}h^+,\quad P^+=(J^+)^{-1}.\end{aligned}\]

3 · Expose the Kalman correction

\[\begin{aligned}\nu&=z-Hm^-,\quad S=HP^-H^\mathsf T+R,\\(J^+)^{-1}H^\mathsf T R^{-1}&=P^-H^\mathsf T S^{-1}=K,\\m^+&=m^-+K\nu,\\P^+&=P^--KSK^\mathsf T.\end{aligned}\]

4 · BLUE: a different experiment

\[\begin{gathered}d=\begin{bmatrix}a\\z\end{bmatrix},\quad G=\begin{bmatrix}I\\H\end{bmatrix},\quad W=\operatorname{diag}(P^-,R),\\\hat x=(G^\mathsf T W^{-1}G)^{-1}G^\mathsf T W^{-1}d\\=a+K(z-Ha).\end{gathered}\]

Here \(a=x+\epsilon\), \(x\) is fixed; errors are zero-mean and uncorrelated with covariances \(P^-,R\).

Family 04 / key idea

KL variational updating

Key equations →

Idea. Optimize a whole density. Penalize departure from the prior with KL divergence, and reward explaining the new observation through its expected negative log likelihood.

\[q_* = \arg\min_{q\ge0,\,\int q=1}\left\{D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\Vert p^-)+\mathbb E_q[-\log p(z\mid x)]\right\}.\]

What makes this different to least squares?

Least squares varies the state or mean. This problem varies the full density—including its covariance. In the Gaussian reduction, the entropy term is what determines the covariance, rather than collapsing the answer to a point.

What has been merged

“Minimum surprise” and relative-entropy language are grouped here, but the objective or constraints must be explicit. The displayed likelihood-loss objective recovers Bayes exactly; the cited maximum-relative-entropy paper uses a related constrained formulation.

Essential correction: minimizing KL to the prior alone returns the prior. The measurement likelihood/loss or a specified observation constraint is indispensable.

[6] Bissiri, Holmes & Walker (2016) · [7] Giffin & Urniezius (2014)09 / 14

Family 04 / one-page equation summary

KL variational updating

← Key idea

For one update: \(x\sim\mathcal N(m^-,P^-)\), \(z=Hx+v\), \(v\sim\mathcal N(0,R)\), with independent errors; \(P^-\succ0,\ R\succ0\).

1 · Optimize over densities

\[\begin{aligned}\ell_z(x)&=-\log p(z\mid x),\\\mathcal F(q)&=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\Vert p^-)+\mathbb E_q[\ell_z(x)].\end{aligned}\]

Include the likelihood normalizing constant in ℓ. Minimize over \(q\ge0\), \(\int q=1\).

2 · Identify the exact minimizer

\[\begin{aligned}\mathcal F(q)&=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q\Vert p(x\mid z))-\log p(z),\\q_*(x)&\propto p^-(x)e^{-\ell_z(x)}\\&=p^-(x)p(z\mid x).\end{aligned}\]

3 · Gaussian parameter objective

\[\begin{aligned}q&=\mathcal N(\mu,\Sigma),\quad\Sigma\succ0,\\J&=(P^-)^{-1}+H^\mathsf T R^{-1}H,\\\mathcal F(\mu,\Sigma)&=\tfrac12\|\mu-m^-\|_{(P^-)^{-1}}^2\\&\quad+\tfrac12\|z-H\mu\|_{R^{-1}}^2\\&\quad+\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(J\Sigma)-\tfrac12\log\det\Sigma+c.\end{aligned}\]

4 · Recover mean AND covariance

\[\begin{aligned}h&=(P^-)^{-1}m^-+H^\mathsf T R^{-1}z,\\\nabla_\mu\mathcal F=0&\Rightarrow J\mu=h,\\\nabla_\Sigma\mathcal F=0&\Rightarrow \Sigma^{-1}=J,\\m^+&=J^{-1}h,\quad P^+=J^{-1}.\end{aligned}\]

\(c\) is independent of \(\mu,\Sigma\). Gaussian optimization is exact here, not an approximation.

[6] Bissiri, Holmes & Walker (2016) · [7] Giffin & Urniezius (2014)10 / 14

Computational notes / not a fifth derivation

Gaussian elimination: the computational form

\(a\sim\mathcal N(m_a,P_a)\), \(b=Fa+w\), \(z=Hb+v\), with independent \(w\sim\mathcal N(0,Q)\), \(v\sim\mathcal N(0,R)\). Here \(P_a,Q,R\succ0\); omit known control offsets.

1 · Write the joint canonical Gaussian

\[\begin{gathered}u=\begin{bmatrix}a\\b\end{bmatrix},\quad\Lambda=\begin{bmatrix}A&C\\C^\mathsf T&D\end{bmatrix},\quad\eta=\begin{bmatrix}\eta_a\\\eta_b\end{bmatrix},\\p(u)\propto\exp\!\left[-\tfrac12 u^\mathsf T\Lambda u+u^\mathsf T\eta\right].\end{gathered}\]

2 · Blocks from the transition factor

\[\begin{aligned}A&=P_a^{-1}+F^\mathsf T Q^{-1}F,\\C&=-F^\mathsf T Q^{-1},\quad D=Q^{-1},\\\eta_a&=P_a^{-1}m_a,\quad\eta_b=0.\end{aligned}\]

3 · Eliminate a: the prediction message

\[\begin{aligned}J^-&=D-C^\mathsf T A^{-1}C,\\h^-&=\eta_b-C^\mathsf T A^{-1}\eta_a,\\P^-&=(J^-)^{-1}=FP_aF^\mathsf T+Q,\\m^-&=P^-h^-=Fm_a.\end{aligned}\]

4 · Add the observation factor

\[\begin{aligned}J^+&=J^-+H^\mathsf T R^{-1}H,\\h^+&=h^-+H^\mathsf T R^{-1}z,\\P^+&=(J^+)^{-1},\quad m^+=P^+h^+.\end{aligned}\]

The same reduced quadratic follows from minimizing over the old state instead of integrating it out.

Connections / do not count again

Numerical forms and control duality

QR: implement the least-squares family

\[\begin{gathered}P^-=L_pL_p^\mathsf T,\quad R=L_rL_r^\mathsf T,\\\mathcal A=\begin{bmatrix}L_p^{-1}\\L_r^{-1}H\end{bmatrix},\quad b=\begin{bmatrix}L_p^{-1}m^-\\L_r^{-1}z\end{bmatrix},\\\mathcal A=UT,\quad U^\mathsf T U=I,\\Tm^+=U^\mathsf T b,\quad P^+=T^{-1}T^{-\mathsf T}.\end{gathered}\]

Use triangular solves. Avoiding explicit normal equations avoids squaring the condition number of the stacked system.

Riccati / LQR: a consequence and duality

\[\begin{aligned}\Pi_{k+1}&=F\Pi_kF^\mathsf T+Q\\&\quad-F\Pi_kH^\mathsf T(H\Pi_kH^\mathsf T+R)^{-1}H\Pi_kF^\mathsf T,\\\Pi_k&=P_k^-.\end{aligned}\]

For constant matrices, substitute the optimal gain and predict again. The LQR dual uses \(A_c=F^\mathsf T, B_c=H^\mathsf T\); finite-horizon time directions are reversed. A constant gain additionally needs convergence.

RLS is a special case: for a static state \(F=I,Q=0\), accumulate \(J_k=J_{k-1}+H_k^\mathsf TR_k^{-1}H_k\) and \(h_k=h_{k-1}+H_k^\mathsf TR_k^{-1}z_k\). With dynamics, predict first; process precision does not simply add.

What survives consolidation

Same answer; different assumptions

Under the linear–Gaussian model

\[\text{posterior mean}=\text{MAP}=\text{LMMSE}\]

Bayes computes the posterior. MSE projection and least squares recover its mean. Graph elimination, information form and QR reorganize its computation. KL expresses the same posterior as a variational optimum.

Outside that model

With non-Gaussian noise, LMMSE need not be the posterior mean; MAP need not equal that mean either. Correlated noises require modified cross-covariances. Nonlinear models do not preserve these exact equivalences.

One scalar sanity check

\[\begin{gathered}m^-=0,\ P^-=4,\ H=1,\ z=3,\ R=1,\\S=5,\quad K=0.8,\quad m^+=2.4,\quad P^+=0.8.\end{gathered}\]

Every applicable route returns the same numbers.
