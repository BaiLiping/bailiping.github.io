Estimation notes / a one-page reference

One filter.
Many derivations.

The Kalman filter is simultaneously a Gaussian conditioning rule, an orthogonal projection and a recursive quadratic optimization. Here are the main routes, with the algebra that makes them meet.

Different routes, not ten different filters. Some start from a different optimality principle; others reorganize the same calculation. Gaussian assumptions give the exact posterior. With only the stated first and second moments, the Kalman recursion is the best affine estimator, not generally the best nonlinear estimator.[1][2]

Start here

The model and the common answer

Linear state-space model
\[\begin{aligned}x_k&=F_kx_{k-1}+B_ku_k+w_k,&w_k&\sim\N(0,Q_k),\\z_k&=H_kx_k+v_k,&v_k&\sim\N(0,R_k).\end{aligned}\]

The initial state is Gaussian; noises are independent across time, of each other and of the initial state. Controls are known. The displayed inverse-based formulas assume \(P_k^-\succ0\) and \(R_k\succ0\); \(Q_k\succeq0\).

\(x_k\in\mathbb R^n\): state; \(z_k\in\mathbb R^r\): observation.

\(F_k\): transition; \(H_k\): observation matrix.

\((m_k^-,P_k^-)\): estimate before observing \(z_k\).

\((m_k^+,P_k^+)\): estimate after observing \(z_k\).

The recursion every route recovers
Predict · propagate the previous estimate
\[\begin{aligned}m_k^-&=F_km_{k-1}^++B_ku_k,\\P_k^-&=F_kP_{k-1}^+F_k\T+Q_k.\end{aligned}\]
Correct · suppress k from here onward
\[\begin{aligned}\nu&=z-Hm^-,&S&=HP^-H\T+R,\\K&=P^-H\T S^{-1},&m^+&=m^-+K\nu,\\P^+&=P^--KSK\T.\end{aligned}\]

\(\nu\) is the innovation; \(S\) its covariance; \(K\) the gain. In the Gaussian case, \(p(x\mid z)=\N(x;m^+,P^+)\), with past observations understood.

Starting point: Kalman (1960) [1]; Särkkä, Chapter 4 [2]. Below, \(\|a\|_W^2=a\T Wa\); superscripts − and + mean before and after, not inversion.

Ten routes to the update

The key idea, decisive equations and assumptions for each route.

01Probability

Recursive Bayes + completing the square

Idea. Predict by integrating the transition model; correct by multiplying the prior and likelihood. Linear–Gaussian models keep both operations Gaussian.

\[\begin{aligned}p_k^-(x)&=\int p(x\mid x')p_{k-1}^+(x')\,dx',\\p_k^+(x)&\propto p(z_k\mid x)\,p_k^-(x).\end{aligned}\]

For one correction, collect the quadratic and linear terms in \(x\):

\[\begin{aligned}p^+(x)&\propto\exp\!\left[-\tfrac12 x\T J^+x+x\T h^+\right],\\J^+&=(P^-)^{-1}+H\T R^{-1}H,\\h^+&=(P^-)^{-1}m^-+H\T R^{-1}z,\\P^+&=(J^+)^{-1},\qquad m^+=P^+h^+.\end{aligned}\]

Key step. Complete the square around \((J^+)^{-1}h^+\). The prediction integral gives the moment propagation above. “HMM forward recursion” and “recursive Bayesian filtering” describe this same route.

Reference: Särkkä (2013), §§4.2–4.3 [2].

02Probability

Joint Gaussian conditioning

Idea. Write the state and measurement as one Gaussian vector, then condition its first block on its second.

\[\begin{bmatrix}x\\z\end{bmatrix}\sim\N\!\left(\begin{bmatrix}m^-\\Hm^-\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}P^-&P^-H\T\\HP^-&S\end{bmatrix}\right).\]
\[\begin{aligned}m^+&=m^-+\underbrace{P^-H\T S^{-1}}_{K}(z-Hm^-),\\P^+&=P^--P^-H\T S^{-1}HP^-.\end{aligned}\]

Key step. The conditional covariance is a Schur complement. This is the shortest route when Gaussian conditioning is already familiar.

Reference: Särkkä (2013), Gaussian identities and §4.3 [2].

03Estimation geometry

LMMSE, orthogonality & innovations

Idea. Among estimates \(m^-+K\nu\), the least-squares error must be orthogonal to the information used to construct the estimate.

\[\begin{aligned}e^-&=x-m^-,\quad e^+=e^--K\nu,\\0&=\E[e^+\nu\T]=P^-H\T-KS,\\\Longrightarrow\quad K&=P^-H\T S^{-1}.\end{aligned}\]

Why it is recursive. The innovation is orthogonal to all earlier observations after their affine projection has been removed. Add only this new component. This connects the Kalman filter to causal Wiener filtering; Gaussianity is not required for linear minimum mean-square error (LMMSE) optimality.

References: Kalman (1960) [1]; Kailath’s innovations approach (1968) [3].

04Estimation / optimization

Direct minimum-covariance derivation

Idea. Write the error covariance for an arbitrary gain and optimize it directly. Independent measurement noise gives the Joseph expression:

\[\begin{aligned}P(K)&=(I-KH)P^-(I-KH)\T+KRK\T,\\\nabla_K\tr P(K)&=2(KS-P^-H\T)=0.\end{aligned}\]

The resulting \(K_*=P^-H\T S^{-1}\) minimizes not just the trace, but the covariance in the positive-semidefinite order:

\[P(K)=P(K_*)+(K-K_*)S(K-K_*)\T\succeq P(K_*).\]

Key insight. Any other gain adds a nonnegative error term. Under Gaussian errors, minimizing this covariance also minimizes error entropy, \(h(e^+)=\tfrac12\log\!\big((2\pi e)^n\det P(K)\big)\). This is an error-entropy interpretation, distinct from the KL update in route 09.

References: Kalman (1960) [1]; information-theoretic treatment by Kalata & Priemer (1979) [4].

05Optimization

MAP as weighted least squares

Idea. Minimize the negative log posterior: moving away from the prediction and disagreeing with the observation both incur covariance-weighted costs. Use \(J^+,h^+\) from route 01.

\[\phi(x)=\tfrac12\|x-m^-\|_{(P^-)^{-1}}^2+\tfrac12\|z-Hx\|_{R^{-1}}^2.\]
\[\begin{aligned}\nabla\phi(x)&=J^+x-h^+=0,\\m^+&=m^-+(J^+)^{-1}H\T R^{-1}\nu,\\(J^+)^{-1}H\T R^{-1}&=P^-H\T S^{-1}=K.\end{aligned}\]

Key step. Solve the normal equations; the matrix identity exposes the gain. For this Gaussian quadratic, the mode equals the mean, and \(P^+=(\nabla^2\phi)^{-1}\) exactly. For a nonlinear or non-Gaussian posterior, neither equality is automatic.

Reference: Gaussian estimation and least-squares formulation in Loeliger et al. (2007) [5].

06Unbiased estimation

BLUE / Gauss–Markov

Idea. In an alternative deterministic-state model, combine two unbiased observations: \(a=x+\epsilon\), with covariance \(P^-\), and \(z=Hx+v\), with covariance \(R\). Their errors are uncorrelated.

\[d=\begin{bmatrix}a\\z\end{bmatrix},\quad G=\begin{bmatrix}I\\H\end{bmatrix},\quad W=\begin{bmatrix}P^-&0\\0&R\end{bmatrix}.\]
\[\begin{aligned}\hat x_{\mathrm{BLUE}}&=(G\T W^{-1}G)^{-1}G\T W^{-1}d\\&=a+K(z-Ha),\\\Cov(\hat x-x)&=(G\T W^{-1}G)^{-1}=P^+.\end{aligned}\]

What changes. The same algebra now expresses best linear unbiased estimation, without a distributional prior or Gaussian requirement.

Here \(a\) is a random unbiased observation of the fixed state. A fixed Bayesian prior mean \(m^-\) is not unbiased for every possible \(x\); the two statistical interpretations should not be conflated.

Reference: Aitken’s generalized least-squares result (1936) [6].

07Information / recursion

Information form, RLS & Woodbury

Idea. Store precision \(J^-=(P^-)^{-1}\) and information vector \(h^-=J^-m^-\). Independent evidence adds rather than re-solving the entire history.

\[J^+=J^-+H\T R^{-1}H,\qquad h^+=h^-+H\T R^{-1}z.\]

Woodbury converts the updated precision back to a covariance using an observation-sized solve:

\[\begin{aligned}(J^-+H\T R^{-1}H)^{-1}&=P^--P^-H\T S^{-1}HP^-,\\m^+&=m^-+P^-H\T S^{-1}(z-Hm^-).\end{aligned}\]

Connection to RLS. Ordinary recursive least squares for a static unknown is the special case \(F=I,\ Q=0\), with an initial quadratic prior. With dynamics, first predict: \(J_k^-=(F_kP_{k-1}^+F_k\T+Q_k)^{-1}\). Process information does not simply add in the same way as measurement information.

Reference: Gaussian information parameters and recursive least squares in Loeliger et al. (2007) [5].

08Graphical models

Factor graphs & block elimination

Idea. Factor the trajectory distribution into local Gaussian terms. Passing a forward message means eliminating the old state and retaining only a Gaussian in the current state.

\[p(x_{0:k},z_{1:k})=p(x_0)\prod_{i=1}^{k}p(x_i\mid x_{i-1})p(z_i\mid x_i).\]

If a joint Gaussian has precision and information vector

\[\Lambda=\begin{bmatrix}A&C\\C\T&D\end{bmatrix},\qquad\eta=\begin{bmatrix}\eta_a\\\eta_b\end{bmatrix},\]

integrating out block \(a\) leaves a message to block \(b\):

\[J_b=D-C\T A^{-1}C,\qquad h_b=\eta_b-C\T A^{-1}\eta_a.\]

Key step. Elimination produces the prediction; adding the observation factor produces the correction. Forward messages give filtering; a backward pass adds smoothing. This is a Schur complement of precision for marginalization, not route 02’s Schur complement of covariance for conditioning.

The connection to optimization and dynamic programming

Minimizing the joint quadratic over \(a\) yields the same reduced quadratic in \(b\) as Gaussian integration, up to a constant independent of \(b\). Thus sum–product, quadratic elimination and a dynamic-programming cost-to-go recover the same filtered mean here. The Gaussian normalization is needed when tracking evidence, not just the mean and covariance.

Reference: Loeliger et al. (2007), Gaussian message passing [5].

09Information theory

KL divergence & variational Bayes

Idea. Balance staying close to the prior against expected measurement surprise. Let \(\ell_z(x)=-\log p(z\mid x)\), including its normalizing constant, and optimize over probability densities:

\[q_* = \underset{q\geq0,\,\int q=1}{\arg\min}\;\underbrace{\KL(q\|p^-)+\E_q[\ell_z(x)]}_{\mathcal F(q)}.\]
\[\begin{aligned}\mathcal F(q)&=\KL\big(q\|p(x\mid z)\big)-\log p(z),\\\Longrightarrow\quad q_*(x)&\propto p^-(x)e^{-\ell_z(x)}=p^-(x)p(z\mid x).\end{aligned}\]

Key step. Nonnegativity of KL makes the exact posterior the minimizer. With the Gaussian prior and likelihood, its parameters are precisely \(J^+,h^+\) from route 01. This is a variational expression of Bayes, not an approximation.

Recover both the mean and covariance explicitly

Set \(q=\N(\mu,\Sigma)\), which contains the exact answer in this model. Omitting only constants independent of \(\mu,\Sigma\):

\[\begin{aligned}\mathcal F(\mu,\Sigma)=\tfrac12\big[&\|\mu-m^-\|_{(P^-)^{-1}}^2+\|z-H\mu\|_{R^{-1}}^2\\&+\tr(J^+\Sigma)-\log\det\Sigma\big]+c.\end{aligned}\]
\[\nabla_\mu\mathcal F=0\Rightarrow J^+\mu=h^+,\qquad\nabla_\Sigma\mathcal F=0\Rightarrow\Sigma^{-1}=J^+.\]

The quadratic terms determine the mean; the trace–log-determinant balance determines the covariance.

Minimizing \(\KL(q\|p^-)\) alone returns the unchanged prior. Measurement information must enter through a likelihood/loss term or explicitly specified constraints. Maximum-relative-entropy derivations use such constraints; “minimize surprise” by itself is not a complete derivation.

Variational updating: Bissiri, Holmes & Walker (2016) [7]. Related constrained-entropy Kalman derivation: Giffin & Urniezius (2014) [8].

10Numerical linear algebra

Square-root information filtering / QR

Idea. Whiten and stack the two residuals from route 05, then solve by an orthogonal factorization instead of forming the normal matrix.

\[\begin{gathered}P^-=L_pL_p\T,\quad R=L_rL_r\T,\\\mathcal A=\begin{bmatrix}L_p^{-1}\\L_r^{-1}H\end{bmatrix},\qquad b=\begin{bmatrix}L_p^{-1}m^-\\L_r^{-1}z\end{bmatrix}.\end{gathered}\]

Use the thin QR factorization \(\mathcal A=UT\), where \(U\T U=I\) and \(T\) is upper triangular:

\[T m^+=U\T b,\qquad J^+=T\T T,\qquad P^+=T^{-1}T^{-\mathsf T}.\]

What changes. The statistical answer is identical, but the computation uses triangular factors. Avoiding explicit normal equations avoids squaring the condition number of the stacked system. Interpret the inverses above as triangular solves in code.

Reference: Kaminski, Bryson & Schmidt (1971), square-root filtering survey [9].

Related views

Two more names you will encounter

Useful connections, rather than additional independent measurement-update derivations.

Gaussian-process regression

A linear–Gaussian state-space model induces a temporal Gaussian process. Condition its current state on the new observation using \(K_{xz}=P^-H\T\) and \(K_{zz}=S\): this is route 02. An arbitrary GP kernel need not have an exact finite-dimensional Markov realization; some state-space representations are approximations.[10]

Riccati recursion & LQR duality

For constant \(F,H,Q,R\), substitute the optimal gain into the covariance recursion. With \(\Pi_k=P_k^-\):

\[\begin{aligned}\Pi_{k+1}={}&F\Pi_kF\T+Q\\&-F\Pi_kH\T(H\Pi_kH\T+R)^{-1}H\Pi_kF\T.\end{aligned}\]

This is a discrete Riccati map. It is transpose-dual to the linear-quadratic regulator (LQR) Riccati equation, using control matrices \(A_c=F\T,\ B_c=H\T\) and costs \(Q_c=Q,\ R_c=R\); the finite-horizon recursions run in opposite time directions. A steady-state gain requires convergence conditions, not merely constant matrices.[1]

The unifying picture

What is actually equivalent?

Gaussian conditioning = quadratic optimization = affine projection.

Under the linear–Gaussian assumptions: the posterior mean, MAP estimate and LMMSE estimate coincide. Information form, elimination and QR reorganize the same solution; they do not introduce new evidence.

Outside those assumptions: keep the distinctions. The covariance-based recursion can remain LMMSE without being the exact Bayesian filter. Correlated noises require modified formulas. Nonlinear models lead to approximations rather than automatic equivalence.[1][2]

Implementation: solve \(S K\T=HP^-\) rather than explicitly invert \(S\). Use the Joseph form from route 04 or a square-root implementation to protect covariance structure numerically.[9]

Scalar check: \(m^-=0,\ P^-=4,\ H=1,\ z=3,\ R=1\) gives \(K=0.8,\ m^+=2.4,\ P^+=0.8\) by every applicable route.
