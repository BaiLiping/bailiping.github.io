01 / The approach

Estimate the objects.
Preserve their identities.

A detector produces boxes for each frame. A tracker must decide which boxes belong to existing objects, which indicate new arrivals, and which observations to reject. RFS models also represent uncertainty in how many objects exist.

UNDETECTED OBJECTS

The Poisson component

Represents objects that may exist but have not yet been detected. The birth model introduces the possibility of new objects.

DETECTED OBJECTS

Bernoulli tracks

Each candidate track carries a probability of existence and a distribution over its state. A missing detection reduces confidence without necessarily ending the track.

ASSOCIATION UNCERTAINTY

Competing explanations

A PMBM filter retains a mixture of association hypotheses. The repository’s PMBMGNN variant propagates the highest-weight global hypothesis to reduce that branching.

  1. 01Load detections

    Boxes, class labels, scores, and velocity estimates in the nuScenes format.

  2. 02Predict tracks

    Propagate position, velocity, survival, and possible object births.

  3. 03Associate & update

    Gate candidate matches, update tracks, and manage alternative assignments.

  4. 04Evaluate & inspect

    Export identities and boxes, calculate metrics, and examine scene-level errors.

Implementation references: nuScenes runner and PMBMGNN procedure.

What this work covers. The repository collects classical and RFS tracking implementations, simulation experiments, and a nuScenes application. The main contribution presented here is the experimental workflow and visual diagnosis. The filter families and their underlying theory have established prior implementations.

Read accuracy and identity together.

The log reports 602 identity switches and 286 track fragmentations. Those counts motivate a closer look at individual trajectories even when the average accuracy is strong.

Class differences matter. Bus AMOTA is 0.873, while trailer AMOTA is 0.529. The aggregate score hides substantial variation across object classes.

AMOTA averages recall-normalized tracking accuracy over recall thresholds. AMOTP summarizes position error over recall thresholds. Consult the nuScenes metric definitions for the exact evaluation protocol.

The log’s ID-switch and fragmentation counts are the evaluator’s reported operating-point statistics; they are not averaged in the same way as AMOTA.

03 / Visual diagnosis

Where identity breaks.

The notes are organized below by failure mechanism. Each example distinguishes the recorded observation from the interpretation or improvement it suggests.

01 · Missed detection

Occlusion creates a gap in the evidence.

The original example marks 13 frames of missed pedestrian detection. With no matching measurement, the tracker must balance survival against the chance that the object has left.

Implication: examine existence decay and track management across the gap, as well as the quality of the detections.

02 · Birth versus continuation

A new identity can win over an existing track.

The notes associate high birth rates with premature termination and restart. Lowering the rate to 0.001 fixes several selected examples, including this pair.

Trade-off: making new tracks less likely can also encourage incorrect associations to old tracks.

03 · Motion mismatch

Turning remains difficult after tuning.

Several examples terminate during a curved trajectory. One caption explicitly notes that the problem remains after radar velocity is added.

Hypothesis to test: a motion model that better represents turns may help where changing only the birth rate does not.

04 · Unstable class labels

The object persists.
Its detection label changes.

A car may be detected as a truck, or alternate between other categories. The repository’s class-specific runner makes these errors relevant to association and track continuity.

Direction in the notes: use classification information over time. The nuScenes output still requires a consistent class label within a track.

05 · Ambiguous association

Crossings put identities under pressure.

The notes document identity changes when tracks intersect, including an example of two bus tracks that terminate and restart.

Implication: inspect the competing associations around the intersection, including tracks that temporarily have no detection.

A local repair can hurt the overall score. The notes report a drop from 0.707 to 0.69 after lowering the birth rate: some broken tracks improve, but incorrect associations become more common. This is an earlier tuning observation, separate from the 0.761 validation run above.

04 / Method comparison

Different approximations,
different failure modes.

The source compares outputs labeled MHT and TO-PMB (track-oriented Poisson multi-Bernoulli). These paired visual examples describe individual behavior; they do not establish an overall ranking.

Hypothesis selection

The notes identify early termination and sudden position changes in selected MHT outputs. Inspect which association history is retained when several explanations compete.

Coalescence and long links

A separate group records track coalescence and associations made over large distances. Preserving a track is useful only when the identity and trajectory remain credible.

05 / Lessons and next experiments

Turn observations into
specific tests.

The archive suggests several directions. They are presented as research questions, not as demonstrated improvements.

  1. Adapt birth behavior to the scene.The notes propose stronger birth intensity in crowded regions and lower intensity in sparse regions. Compare against fixed rates using identical detections and evaluation settings.
  2. Accumulate class evidence over time.Test whether a temporal class model reduces the documented label-change failures without introducing incorrect identities.
  3. Revisit motion and survival through gaps.Separate turning errors from occlusion errors; measure continuity and incorrect associations as well as the average score.
  4. Preserve experiment provenance.Save the detector checkpoint, tracker settings, split, code revision, and per-class metrics with every new run so that visual examples and scores can be compared reliably.
